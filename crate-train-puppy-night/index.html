How to Crate Train a Puppy at Night: A Step-by-Step Plan

Your puppy's first nights home can feel endless: crying, whining, and 2 a.m. potty breaks. Here's the reassuring truth — nighttime fussing doesn't mean crate training is failing. It means your puppy is a baby in a strange place, and they need a plan. This is that plan.

Before night one: set the crate up right

A crate only works if the puppy sees it as a bedroom, not a prison. Get these basics right before the first night:

  • Right size: big enough to stand, turn, and lie down — no bigger. Too much space and puppies potty in one corner and sleep in the other. Use the divider panel that comes with most wire crates.
  • Right location: in your bedroom for the first week or two. Hearing you breathe nearby is the single biggest comfort you can offer.
  • Right bedding: something soft, washable, and not too plush (some puppies chew fluffy beds). An old towel is fine to start.
  • Nothing dangly: no collars in the crate, no hanging toys — both are strangulation risks.

The daytime foundation (don't skip this)

Never crate a puppy at night who hasn't had positive crate experiences during the day. Spend 2–3 days on this first:

  1. Feed every meal in the crate with the door open. Meals = the crate predicts good things.
  2. Toss treats in randomly throughout the day. Let the puppy wander in and out freely.
  3. Practice short closed-door sessions — 5 minutes while you're in the room, then 10, then 20. Build gradually.
  4. Add a cue word like "crate" or "bedtime," said cheerfully as you toss a treat inside.

The bedtime routine

Puppies thrive on predictability. Run the same routine every night:

  1. Last water about 2 hours before bed.
  2. Active play 30–45 minutes before bed to burn energy — then calm down together.
  3. Final potty trip right before crating. Boring trip: no play, just business and praise.
  4. Into the crate with a small treat and a safe chew toy. Same cue word, same calm tone, every night.
  5. Lights out. Lie down where the puppy can hear you. Your presence does most of the work.

When the puppy cries: the 3-check rule

Some fussing is normal. When you hear it, run this mental checklist before reacting:

  1. Potty? If it's been 2–3 hours (or it's been a while since the last trip), take them out — boringly, on leash, then straight back in.
  2. Uncomfortable? Too hot, too cold, tangled blanket, needs water earlier in the evening routine?
  3. Just lonely? If checks 1 and 2 are fine, settle them with your voice or a finger through the crate bars. Don't open the door for attention-crying — you'll teach them that crying opens doors.

What NOT to do

  • Don't punish crying. Banging the crate or shouting teaches fear, not quiet.
  • Don't give up on night 2. The first 3–4 nights are the hardest; most puppies turn a corner within a week.
  • Don't move the puppy to your bed "just tonight." Inconsistency confuses them and resets your progress.
  • Don't expect too much too soon. An 8-week-old puppy can hold it roughly 2–3 hours. Plan overnight breaks accordingly.

A realistic timeline

AgeTypical night
8–10 weeks1–2 potty breaks; some crying first few nights
11–14 weeksUsually 1 break; settling faster
15+ weeksMany puppies sleep through (6–7 hours)

Every puppy is different — these are averages, not deadlines. If your puppy is struggling well beyond these ranges, or shows signs of genuine distress (panting, drooling, trying to escape frantically), talk to your vet or a certified trainer. That's not failure; it's good ownership.

Frequently asked questions

How long will my puppy cry in the crate at night?

Most puppies protest for a few nights — typically 10 to 30 minutes of fussing before settling. If crying escalates instead of fading over 3 to 4 nights, slow down: your training steps are too big, or the puppy needs a potty break. Never let a young puppy cry it out for hours; at 8 to 10 weeks they genuinely can't hold it all night.

Where should the crate go at night?

For the first week or two, keep the crate in your bedroom where the puppy can hear and smell you. Proximity reduces panic. Once they're sleeping calmly for several nights, you can gradually move the crate to its permanent spot.

Should I put water in the crate at night?

No. Remove water about two hours before bedtime and offer a final potty trip right before crating. Puppies who drink late at night simply need more overnight potty breaks.
